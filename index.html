Predicting Traffic Decay

By Okay Arda Hamamcı • August 2026
Abstract

Question: To solve FlyRank's core client challenge—helping enterprise SEO teams manage massive, unmonitorable content libraries—can we predict which Page 1 URLs are at imminent risk of severe traffic decay using historical visibility signals? Method: We analyzed 79 million rows of daily Google Search Console performance data across 57 FlyRank enterprise brands, utilizing a Time-Aware Split to train an Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) classifier. Result: The XGBoost model successfully isolated non-linear risk thresholds, outperforming a human heuristic baseline with 66% precision. The resulting output is a sorted, human-readable Action Playbook designed to automate FlyRank's editorial triage, flagging vulnerable, high-value assets for immediate refresh before their traffic crashes.

1. Introduction & Problem Framing

For FlyRank's enterprise clients, ranking on Page 1 of Google is not a permanent state; content naturally decays over time as competitors update their pages and search intent evolves. The core business problem is scale: human editorial teams have limited capacity and simply cannot manually monitor tens of thousands of pages to detect the early, subtle signs of decay.

This research serves as a case study for automating FlyRank's weekly triage process. By leveraging machine learning, we aim to automatically identify the exact high-priority pages that require immediate intervention (e.g., meta-title updates or content refreshes). A false positive costs 15 minutes of a FlyRank editor's time, but a false negative (missing a crash) costs the client thousands of organic sessions.

2. Data & Exclusions

The analysis leverages the FlyRank Internship Warehouse (`fact_content_daily_performance` parquet files), representing 79 million rows of daily search performance across 57 distinct brands.

  • Window: We utilized March 2026 data as our primary evaluation window, reserving February 2026 exclusively for training.
  • Safety & Leakage: We explicitly excluded `client_hash_id` and `content_hash_id` from model training. These are pseudonymous grouping IDs that carry no predictive power and risk overfitting. No client-identifying data is present in this research.

3. Methodology

We engineered a focused dataset to prevent Target Leakage and ensure maximum reproducibility.

  • Features: Total Impressions, Total Clicks, Click-Through Rate (CTR), and Average Position.
  • Target Label: "Decline" (1) if a page loses >20% of its total impressions in the subsequent 30-day window, otherwise (0).
  • Validation Design: We used a strict Time-Aware Split (Train on February, Test on March). This perfectly mimics real-world deployment. A standard random split was audited and discarded because it leaks future data into the training set, artificially inflating accuracy.
  • Baseline: A handwritten heuristic rule assuming "Decline" if a page ranks on Page 1 (Pos <= 10) but captures <1% CTR.

4. Results vs Baseline

Evaluated on the exact same Time-Aware Split (Predicting April declines using March features), the XGBoost model significantly outperformed the human baseline.

5. Limitations

Observational Context Only

This is an observational study. We cannot claim a causal relationship between CTR and Google's ranking algorithm. Furthermore, the model is entirely "blind" to search intent and seasonality. It cannot distinguish between a genuinely failing page and a holiday guide naturally exiting its peak season. It is a prioritization engine, not an absolute truth.

6. Ranked Recommendations (The Playbook)

The model probabilities are translated into a human-readable triage queue for the editorial team:

1. High-Priority Review for Refresh

Code: ml_decline_risk_high_imp
Assigned to pages with >50% decline probability AND >5,000 monthly impressions. These represent the largest potential traffic losses and must be reviewed immediately.

2. Review for Meta-Title Update

Code: ml_decline_risk_standard
Assigned to pages with >50% decline probability on standard traffic volume. These often indicate a CTR or snippet presentation issue.

Human-in-the-Loop Requirement: This queue must NOT be automated. Humans must review the page to ensure the drop isn't a natural "Zero-Click" search term before deploying new meta-titles.
